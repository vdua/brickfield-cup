# Week 1: Diagnostic + number sense

## This week
Week 1 is mostly a check of where he stands. The teaching part is small: four mental-maths moves that save time and cut careless slips. Aim for speed *and* a habit of glancing at the answer to ask "does that size make sense?".

This guide was written from `docs/sasmo-plan.md`, not from book pages. Match the examples to the book chapter you actually use before teaching.

## Concepts

### Round and adjust
- **Idea:** Change a number to a friendly one (100, 500, 1000), do the easy sum, then fix the difference.
- **Ask:** "Is one of these numbers close to a round number?"
- **Trap:** Adjusting the wrong way. 498 is 2 *less* than 500, so the sum comes out 2 *less* too.
- **Example:** 498 + 367. Use 500 + 367 = 867, then take away 2 to get **865**.

### Halve and double (friendly factors)
- **Idea:** Swap a number for a friendlier one with the same product. 25 is a quarter of 100 and 125 is an eighth of 1000.
- **Ask:** "Can I turn this into a ×10, ×100 or ×1000?"
- **Trap:** Dividing one number but forgetting to multiply the other.
- **Example:** 25 × 36. Think 36 ÷ 4 = 9, then 9 × 100 = **900**. Another: 125 × 48. Think 48 ÷ 8 = 6, then 6 × 1000 = **6000**.

### Multiply by 11
- **Idea:** For a 2-digit number, keep the first and last digits and put their sum in the middle.
- **Ask:** "Is it a 2-digit number times 11?"
- **Trap:** Forgetting to carry when the middle sum is 10 or more.
- **Example:** 47 × 11. Middle is 4 + 7 = 11, so write 4, then 11, then 7, carrying the 1 to get **517**. Check: 47 × 10 + 47 = 517.

### Estimate first
- **Idea:** Round both numbers to find roughly how big the answer must be, then compare it to the exact answer.
- **Ask:** "About how big should this be?"
- **Trap:** Trusting an answer that is the wrong size, such as one digit too many.
- **Example:** 49 × 21 is about 50 × 20 = 1000. The exact answer **1029** is close, so it passes. An answer of 10290 would fail at once.

## Saturday script
Start with the 10-minute before-check and no help. Then teach.

### Worked examples (2–3, with him)
1. **Round and adjust:** 698 + 245. 700 + 245 = 945, minus 2 = 943.
2. **Halve and double:** 25 × 28. 28 ÷ 4 = 7, then 7 × 100 = 700.
3. **Multiply by 11:** 53 × 11. Middle 5 + 3 = 8, so 583.

### Guided practice (he does these, you check)
1. 1000 − 462 = ? (538)
2. 303 + 497 = ? (800)
3. 125 × 24 = ? (3000)
4. 5 × 64 = ? Hint: halve then × 10. (320)
5. 11 × 86 = ? Carry the 1. (946)
6. 36 × 99 = ? Hint: 36 × 100 − 36. (3564)
7. Is 2679 a sensible answer for 61 × 39? (No. About 60 × 40 = 2400; exact is 2379.)
8. He makes up one question for each trick and you solve it with the other method.

## Watch for
- He loses 1 or 2 marks on careless mistakes, so push the "does the size make sense?" check on every answer.
- Make him write one line of working, even on mental questions, for the error notebook.
- For the before-check, sit with him but do not help. Mark it first and note which topics were weak.
- If a topic scores badly, the plan lets that topic repeat another week.
