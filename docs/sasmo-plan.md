## At a glance

A 19-week plan (23 Nov 2026 to early April 2027) at about 4.5 hours a week. It starts at his comfort level and gets harder from January. The main job is to move him from IMO Level-1 style questions to the non-routine, multi-step problems where he currently loses marks in Level 2.

| Likely date | Early April 2027 (not announced yet). The 2026 exam was on 5 April and registration closed on 25 March; fee was ₹1,800 ([PW](https://www.pw.live/olympiad/exams/sasmo-2026-exam-date)) |

**Where he stands now.** He gets Gold in IMO/SOF Level 1 every time but doesn't do as well in Level 2. He finds fractions and decimals, word problems, ratio, speed-distance-time and percentages hardest, and usually drops 1 or 2 marks to careless mistakes.

**What that tells us.** He has the speed and school maths. He has less practice with problems that need a strategy before any calculation. That is what SASMO tests, and what Level 2 tests.

**Strategy in three phases**

1. **Build (Weeks 1–6, Nov–early Jan):** Easy-to-medium questions. Fix his weak topics using the bar-model method, and build calm habits that cut careless errors.
2. **Stretch (Weeks 7–13, Jan–Feb):** Real SASMO-level questions on one heuristic per week: logic, counting, geometry, working backwards and assumption.
3. **Perform (Weeks 14–19, Mar–Apr):** Timed past papers, mocks and fixing errors. Lighter weeks around his school annual exams.

The weekly before-and-after checks set the pace. If he's cruising, we push harder sooner. If a topic doesn't stick, it gets another week.

## Weekly rhythm

Every week runs the same way: a quick check before he learns the topic on Saturday, short weekday practice, and a check after on Sunday. That's about 4.5 hours a week.

| Day | Time | What he does | Parent's role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sat | 90 min | **Before-check** (5 questions, 10 min, no help), then learn the week's topic: 2–3 worked examples with you, then 8–10 guided questions | Sit with him. Mark the before-check first, so you can see what he already knows |
| Sun | 60 min | **After-check** (5 new questions on the same topic, 10 min), then a 30-min mixed set (old topics + 2 stretch puzzles), then 10 min fixing errors | Mark the after-check, then fill in the tracker (Assessment section) |
| Mon | 30 min | 6 practice questions on this week's topic | 5-min look in the evening: score + error log |
| Tue | 30 min | 6 harder questions on this week's topic | 5-min look |
| Wed | 30 min | Fun day: 3 logic or puzzle questions (cricket/Lego/shopping themes) | 5-min look |
| Thu | 30 min | Revision: redo 5 questions he got wrong earlier + 10 mental-maths drills | 5-min look |
| Fri | Off | Rest, cricket | — |

**How it works**

- Use a timer for every session. SASMO gives about 3.6 minutes per question, so he needs to learn pacing as well as accuracy.
- He keeps one **error notebook**: the question, his wrong answer, the type of mistake (careless / didn't know the method / misread), and the right working.
- Weekday work should be doable on his own. If he's stuck for more than 5 minutes, he marks it with a star and moves on, and you go through it together at the weekend.
- From Week 7, Tuesday's set comes from SASMO-level material instead of IMO books.

**When to adjust**

- **Before-check 5/5:** he already knows the topic. Skip the basic examples and go straight to the stretch problems for that topic.
- **After-check 2/5 or less:** repeat the topic next week, and push everything else back by a week. Weeks 6 and 12 have spare room for this.
- **Mock 1 above 70%:** start Stretch-phase material in Week 6, not Week 7, and add one SASMO question a day.
- Once SASMO 2027 dates are announced, move the Week 19 row to the actual exam week.

## Assessment and tracking

There are three levels of check: a before-and-after check every week, a mixed review every Sunday, and a full mock about once a month. Write the scores in the tracker below and the trend will show you where to focus.

**1. Weekly before/after check (online quiz or paper, 10 min each)**

- **Before (Sat):** 5 questions on the new topic, done before any teaching. It shows what he already knows.
- **After (Sun):** 5 different questions of the same type and difficulty. It shows what he learnt.
- **How to read it:** gain = After − Before. A big gain means the teaching worked. A low After score means the topic needs another week. A high Before score means we can skip ahead.

**2. Sunday mixed set (30 min).** 8 questions: 4 from older topics, 2 from this week, and 2 stretch puzzles. This checks whether earlier topics have stuck.

**3. Mocks (Weeks 1, 6, 12, 16, 19).** These follow the full SASMO format: 25 questions in 90 minutes. Mark each question by topic, then compare topics across mocks.

**Targets for a careful start (no award target yet)**

- Weeks 1–6: After-check of 4/5 or more on most topics. Mock 1 at 50% or more.
- Weeks 7–13: After-check of 3/5 or more on SASMO-level questions. Mock 2 at 60% or more.
- Weeks 14–19: Mocks at 70% or more, with no more than 1 careless error per paper.

## Week 1 diagnostic test (paper version)

20 questions, 45 minutes, no calculator. Give it on Saturday 28 November, before any teaching. He should show his working, and if he's stuck for more than 3 minutes he should skip and come back. The questions start easy and get harder, and they cover every SASMO Grade 5 topic area.

1. What comes next: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, \_\_\_ ?
2. Find 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + … + 39.
3. 3/4 of a number is 27. What is 2/3 of the same number?
4. Arrange from smallest to largest: 0.6, 5/8, 7/12, 0.65
5. Aarav and Ben have ₹240 altogether. Aarav has ₹40 more than Ben. How much does Ben have?
6. A batsman scored 45, 62 and 38 in his first three innings. How many runs must he score in the 4th innings to average 50?
7. A cricket bat costs ₹800. The shop gives a 15% discount. What is the price after the discount?
8. A Lego box has red and blue bricks in the ratio 3 : 5. There are 64 bricks. How many more blue bricks than red bricks are there?
9. A car travels 150 km in 2.5 hours. At the same speed, how many hours will it take to travel 210 km?
10. What is the smallest number greater than 1 that leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6?
11. How many whole numbers from 1 to 100 are divisible by 3 or 5 (or both)?
12. AB and BA are 2-digit numbers, with A and B as digits. AB + BA = 121, and A − B = 5. What is AB?
13. Amit, Bala, Chirag and Dev ran a race. Bala finished behind Amit but ahead of Chirag. Dev finished immediately after Amit. Who finished third?
14. How many different 3-digit numbers can be made using the digits 1, 2, 3 and 4 if no digit is repeated?
15. 8 friends meet, and each shakes hands once with every other friend. How many handshakes are there?
16. A rectangle is 12 cm by 8 cm. A 4 cm × 4 cm square is cut from one corner. What is the perimeter of the remaining shape?
17. One angle of a triangle is 60°. Of the other two angles, one is twice the other. What is the largest angle?
18. A 3 × 3 × 3 cube is painted on all sides and then cut into 27 small cubes. How many small cubes have exactly 2 painted faces?
19. I think of a number, multiply it by 3, subtract 7, then divide by 2. The answer is 10. What was my number?
20. A farm has chickens and rabbits. There are 20 heads and 56 legs. How many rabbits are there?

**Reading the result**

- **16–20:** He's already near SASMO level. Start Stretch-phase material from Week 3.
- **11–15:** As expected for a strong IMO student. Follow the plan as written.
- **10 or less:** Spend Weeks 2–6 on the bar-model method and his weak topics before starting puzzles.
- Look at **which topics** he got wrong, not just the total. A wrong answer with correct working counts as a careless error, so log it as one.

## Fixing careless mistakes and moving up from Level 1

His 1–2 lost marks per paper are worth fixing first, because they're the cheapest marks to get back. He gets them back with habits, not with more practice.

**Careless-mistake routine (practise it every session, not only in mocks)**

1. **Underline the question word** (“how many *more*”, “*remaining*”, “*largest*”) before he starts.
2. **Write the units** in the answer (cm, ₹, hours). Unit slips are a common way to lose marks.
3. **Do a sense check**: “Is the answer sensible? Can a share be bigger than the total?”
4. **Check by working backwards** on any question that took more than one step.
5. In mocks, **save the last 10 minutes** to recheck starred answers.

**How Level 2 and SASMO differ from Level 1, and what helps**

- Level-1 questions reward speed. SASMO and Level 2 reward choosing a strategy. For every hard problem, ask him “Which strategy did you use?” (bar model, listing, working backwards, assumption, finding a pattern) before asking for the answer.
- Let him struggle for a while. Hold back hints for the first 5 minutes; most of the learning happens in that time.
- Ask him to explain one solution aloud each weekend. If he can teach it, he can repeat it in the exam.
- Praise his strategy and his checking more than his speed or his score.

**Keeping it fun**

- Wednesday puzzles use his interests: cricket scores and run rates, Lego brick counting and cube nets, shopping and discounts.
- After each mock he can pick a small reward, like a Lego set or a cricket session. Base it on effort and checking, not on marks.
- If he starts to dread sessions, cut the weekdays to 3 per week for two weeks. Being consistent matters more than doing a lot.
